### FIG V2-02/03/04 — `figV2_02_04_theorems.png`

Left: the field contributed by each removal level. Middle: Theorem A — every level carries the same total action $E_0=B_{total}/n$. ### FIG V2-05/06 — `figV2_05_06_synthetic.png`

Left: P(safe) against attack amplitude on a log axis; every controller collapses at large A, and the discrimination happens at A in [2,6]. Right: paired bootstrap of Cantor minus each control over 1920 matched conditions. Cantor beats none/constant/periodic but LOSES to central and to shuffled, and is indistinguishable from center-anchored (d_z=0.049) — H3 and H4 rejected in the synthetic setting.


### FIG V2-07/08 — `figV2_07_08_direction.png`

Left: separability of harmful vs harmless prompts along each estimated direction, per layer (a DIAGNOSTIC — high separability does not imply causal control). Right: the causal test that actually licenses use of the direction. At layer 13, adding the direction raises refusal on harmful prompts from 0.625 to 0.875 and subtracting it drops refusal to 0.375. This is what passes STOP CONDITION A.


### FIG V2-10 — `figV2_10_activation_locations.png`

Control magnitude as a function of the safety margin, at matched total action $\int c\,dr = B_{total}$. The barrier controllers are BLOCKING controllers: past the last gap ($m\ll0$, $r\to1$) their force returns to zero, whereas constant steering keeps pushing. This structural difference dominates the LLM results.
